# Diabetes

## Overview
Diabetes is a chronic condition that affects how the body regulates blood glucose (blood sugar). Glucose is the body's main source of energy, and insulin is a hormone produced by the pancreas that helps glucose enter cells to be used for energy. In diabetes, the body either does not produce enough insulin or cannot effectively use the insulin it produces, resulting in elevated blood glucose levels.

## What insulin does
Insulin is a hormone produced by beta cells in the pancreas that helps regulate blood glucose levels by allowing glucose to enter cells and by controlling glucose production and storage.

## Type 1 diabetes
Type 1 diabetes is an autoimmune condition in which the immune system attacks the insulin-producing beta cells of the pancreas. As a result, the body produces little to no insulin. It can develop at any age and requires insulin treatment.

## Type 2 diabetes
Type 2 diabetes occurs when the body's cells become resistant to insulin (insulin resistance), and the pancreas cannot produce enough insulin to compensate. This leads to elevated blood glucose levels. It is the most common type of diabetes.

## Gestational diabetes
Gestational diabetes develops during pregnancy when the body cannot produce enough insulin to meet increased demands. It typically develops during the second or third trimester and often improves after pregnancy, but it increases the risk of developing Type 2 diabetes later in life.
