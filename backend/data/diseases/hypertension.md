# Hypertension

## Overview
High blood pressure, or hypertension, is a common condition that affects the body's arteries. In hypertension, the force of blood pushing against artery walls is too high. Your heart is working harder than normal to circulate blood.

## Systolic and diastolic blood pressure
Blood pressure consists of two numbers. Systolic blood pressure (the top number) is the pressure on your artery walls when your heart contracts. Diastolic blood pressure (the bottom number) is the pressure between beats when your heart relaxes. Blood pressure is measured in millimeters of mercury (mm Hg). According to the American College of Cardiology and the American Heart Association, normal blood pressure is lower than 120/80. In the U.S., healthcare providers define high blood pressure as a top number (systolic) of 130 mm Hg or higher, or a bottom number (diastolic) of 80 mm Hg or higher.

## Blood pressure categories
The American College of Cardiology and the American Heart Association divide blood pressure into four general categories.
- Normal blood pressure: lower than 120/80 mm Hg.
- Elevated blood pressure: the top number ranges from 120 to 129 mm Hg and the bottom number is below 80 mm Hg.
- Stage 1 hypertension: the top number ranges from 130 to 139 mm Hg or the bottom number is between 80 and 89 mm Hg.
- Stage 2 hypertension: the top number is 140 mm Hg or higher or the bottom number is 90 mm Hg or higher.

## Types of hypertension
Primary hypertension: the cause could be genetic or from past family history. Secondary hypertension: the cause could be an underlying medication or medical condition.

## Lifestyle changes
- Quit smoking.
- Exercise daily.
- Eat a healthy diet. A diet rich in leafy greens such as spinach and kale can help relax the arteries. Berries contain high amounts of antioxidants. Nuts and seeds are also good choices.
